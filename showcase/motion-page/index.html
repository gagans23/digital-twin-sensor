The thesis

Context is not a bigger prompt. It is a living system of evidence, boundaries, and memory.

This piece explores a product direction for personal digital twins: collect the least invasive signals that help a human or agent resume work, then make every inference visible, audited, and reversible. A Safari research session, an editor burst, and a GitLab handoff become a privacy-scored working sphere instead of a raw activity log.

Motion concept

The twin is shown as a breathing graph, not a profile card.

The motion language is quiet and instrument-like: signals orbit, privacy gates pulse, and context packs leave the graph only after passing through an admission boundary.

Design attachment

A single visual grammar: paper, trace, ring, gate.

The page avoids generic AI-site signals by using one symbolic object, restrained motion, editorial spacing, and marginalia-style labels. It should feel designed by someone who has handled enterprise systems, not assembled from a template.

Product Ops

Always-on, but never invisible.

Doctor checks, watchdog state, sample freshness, permission posture, privacy gates, and research gaps are exposed in the UI.

Human Control

Pause, resume, and purge.

The sensor can stay installed while collection is paused. Expired rows can be deleted under the retention policy.

Agent Handoff

Summary-only context packs.

Instead of exporting the raw ledger, the system emits purpose-gated packs for coding agents, issue handoff, or local review.

Research spark

Inspired by X-SYNTH and the context-engineering shift.

X-SYNTH argues that enterprise context synthesis is not only a retrieval problem. It is a relevance problem grounded in observed human attention, behavioral baselines, and task-specific filters. This product prototype asks what that looks like as a local-first, user-controlled personal context system.
